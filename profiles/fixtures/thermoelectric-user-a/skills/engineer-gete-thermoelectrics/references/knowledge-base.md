## 1. Structure and native defects

GeTe is a narrow-gap IV–VI compound commonly described at room temperature as rhombohedrally distorted rocksalt, evolving toward an average cubic rocksalt structure at elevated temperature. The apparent transition temperature, breadth, hysteresis, and local distortion depend on composition, defects, carrier population, thermal history, and probe; do not treat one quoted temperature as universal or the transition mechanism as settled.

Low formation energy of acceptor-like Ge vacancies is consistent with conventional Ge-deficient GeTe being strongly p-type. Specific studies report Hall hole concentrations of order `10^21 cm^-3`; use this only as context and measure each batch. “Two holes per vacancy” is formal charge counting, not a direct vacancy-concentration measurement. Combine final composition, Hall field sweeps, phase/defect-sensitive experiments, and calculations.

## 2. Electronic and thermal levers

Carrier compensation can increase `|S|` and reduce electronic heat conduction while sacrificing conductivity/mobility. Compare PF and quality-factor behavior through temperature and at matched carrier concentration.

Composition and phase tuning may reduce offsets among valence extrema, but “band convergence” requires phase-aware calculation or spectroscopy and a transport signature beyond ordinary carrier tuning. Interband scattering can erase the expected gain.

Point defects, dislocations, planar vacancy structures, nanodomains, precipitates, grain boundaries, and phase coexistence can scatter phonons. The same features can scatter carriers or destabilize the material. Quantify population/length scale, control density/porosity, and propagate Lorenz and heat-capacity sensitivity before attributing lower lattice `kappa`.

## 3. Composition strategy

Organize evidence by intended lever and chemistry: near-stoichiometric/Ge-rich; donor or compensating dopants; Sb/Bi-containing families; TAGS/Ag-Sb-Te alloys; Pb-containing high-performance alloys with distinct toxicity policy; Se/S and multicomponent strategies; or emerging n-type claims. Flag carrier type, phase constitution, lead content, temperature window, and final—not nominal—composition.

Track volatility, wall reaction, segregation, precipitates, densification texture, machining, ingot position, batch mass, yield, and process window. Do not call a route scalable from a larger nominal charge alone.

## 4. Device implications

GeTe is principally a mid-temperature p-leg. Select n-leg/segments by compatibility factor, temperature range, CTE, mechanics, contact process, and stability. Structural evolution and differing phase-specific expansion may add thermomechanical stress, but proving crack causality requires cycling and fractography.

Hot-side reactions and diffusion can dominate. Screen barriers/electrodes/bonds for specific GeTe composition, surface preparation, bonding temperature/pressure, contact resistivity, reaction-layer growth, CTE/stress, and mechanical strength before building modules.
